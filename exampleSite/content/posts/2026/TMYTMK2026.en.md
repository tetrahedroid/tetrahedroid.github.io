A new paper from our group has been published.

The solubility of water in liquid CO<sub>2</sub> coexisting with CO<sub>2</sub> hydrate or liquid water is evaluated in order to investigate the thermodynamic conditions to avoid the formation of CO<sub>2</sub> hydrate in transportation processes of liquid CO<sub>2</sub>. To this end, theoretical calculations have been carried out to obtain the chemical potentials of water and CO<sub>2</sub> in all the phases involved in the coexistence. The solubility of water in liquid CO<sub>2</sub> coexisting with liquid water decreases with decreasing temperature over a wide range of temperature and pressure except for the vicinity of the critical point of CO<sub>2</sub>. The decrease in the solubility is further enhanced by formation of hydrate. We estimate the Gibbs energy of hydrate formation, which is an important property for sequestration of CO<sub>2</sub>, for cases where the temperature or pressure of water-saturated liquid CO<sub>2</sub> decreases. We also estimate the amount of water precipitated as hydrate during these processes, which has a direct bearing on flow assurance in CO<sub>2</sub> transportation. The present study will contribute to the development of a low-energy, safe CO₂ transport network aiming at achieving large-scale carbon neutrality.

- H. Tanaka, M. Matsumoto, T. Yagasaki, M. Takeuchi, Y. Mori, and T. Kono, “Phase behaviour of liquid CO<sub>2</sub> with an impurity of water: Influence of CO<sub>2</sub> hydrate,” Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys., **28**, 15002 - 15014 (2026).
